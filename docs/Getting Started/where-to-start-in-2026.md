You have more entry points into One Piece in 2026
than any previous generation of fans.

Below are four ways to start, each suited to
a different level of commitment.

## Your Options

### 🎬 Option 1: The Netflix Live-Action Series (Recommended First Step)

Netflix's live action adaptation covers the
<Glossary>East Blue Saga</Glossary> in 8 episodes.
It is beautiful, faithful to the source material,
and Oda personally supervised it.

**Best for:** Complete newcomers who want to test
the waters before committing to 1155 episodes.

**What to expect:** Great production value, strong
casting, and enough of the story to know if
One Piece is for you.

**After watching:** If you loved it, start the original
anime from Episode 1. You already know the characters
and the world. The original goes much deeper.

### 📺 Option 2: Start The Anime From Episode 1

The original and still the best way to experience
the full story. Nothing is cut, nothing is rushed,
every detail from the manga is there.

**Best for:** People ready to commit to the journey.

**What to expect:** The first episodes are slower
and the animation is older. It picks up quickly.
By the Arlong Park arc (around Episode 31), the
emotional stakes and action ramp up significantly.

**Pro tip:** Use our
[Episodes To Skip](/docs/episodes-to-skip) guide to
save yourself hours of <Glossary>filler</Glossary>.

### 📖 Option 3: Read The Manga

Oda's original vision with his exact artwork.
Faster than the anime and currently ahead of it.

**Best for:** People who prefer reading or want
to get to current events faster.

**Where to start:** Volume 1, Chapter 1.
Available on Viz Media and Manga Plus.

### 🎌 Option 4: Start The One Piece Remake (In Production)

**WIT Studio** — known for Attack on Titan and
Spy x Family — is producing an official remake
for Netflix titled **"The One Piece."**

The anime remake tackles a real barrier: the original
anime's 1,155-episode count and early pacing can
discourage newcomers. It retells the same story with
modern production and a tighter pace.

It starts from the <Glossary>East Blue Saga</Glossary>
with modern animation and a faster pace, addressing
pacing issues from the original series.

**What we know:**

* Studio: WIT Studio
* Platform: Netflix
* Approach: Seasonal, high-quality, modern animation
* Status: Currently in production (as of March 2026).
  No release date announced.

**Best for:** People who want a modern retelling with
updated animation and faster pacing, or who bounced off
the original due to its length or older art style.

**Our honest take:** We recommend starting the
original first. WIT Studio is exceptional, and
early previews already show strong production
quality. But no condensed version can fully capture
28 years of detail, callbacks, and emotional
buildup. Start the original now, then watch the
anime remake as a companion experience once it releases.

**Also coming in 2026:** One Piece: Heroines,
a spin-off anime featuring the women of One Piece.

## Our Recommendation

Start with the **Netflix live-action series**
(8 episodes on Netflix) to get hooked.

Then start the **anime from Episode 1** and use
this guide to navigate your journey.

1,155 episodes sounds impossible. Then you realize
you're watching three at a time without noticing. 🏴‍☠️

## A Note On Patience

Some early episodes move at a slower pace. Oda uses
them to establish character backstories, motivations,
and world-building details that become critical
hundreds of episodes later. The East Blue Saga
introduces the core crew one by one, and each
backstory builds the emotional foundation for
the rest of the series.

Oda plays a very long game. Details introduced
in Episode 1 pay off in Episode 1000.

**The setup is part of the story. Trust it.**
